# bench sift: 2026-09-22, what switching it on costs

Machine: DESKTOP-AHUN9RO, go1.27.1 windows/amd64, 2026-09-22. Credential kind: key. Route: openrouter, `typesafe/jev-1.13-20260917`, for the recorded rows only.

Cost unit: money. A money figure is dollars that left the account behind the credential named above, read from the response of the call it names and never from a rate card.

**No model call and no Jev call was made to write this report.** Every figure is read from `.tofu/sessions` and `.tofu/log`, both read only, or copied from the TOFU-217 log of 2026-09-21 where it says so on its own line. The offline measurement is `TestWhatSwitchingTheShellSiftOnWouldCost` in this package and its output is pasted below whole. No first call was discarded because no call was made.

## The answer

**Switching `shell_sift@1` from shadow to enforced costs a median $0.00054 a session and $0.01107 in the worst session of 109 recorded, and it stops sending the model 54.0 percent of every shell result, which is 13.7 percent of every byte the model reads back from a tool.** The worst recorded session costs about one cent. The latency is the number to argue about: 838 ms added to a shell tool call, from the TOFU-217 log.

The policy still reads `mode: shadow` and this ticket did not change it.

## Dollars per session

Median $0.000540, p95 $0.005508, worst $0.011070, $0.1131 over all 109 recorded sessions.

The per-decision cost is not in the ledger. **The ledger carries 2,999 rows and not one of them is a `shell_sift` decision**, because the point has never run outside a bench. So the figure comes from the TOFU-217 log of 2026-09-21: $0.018 for 594 live Jev decisions over two full runs of the 34 row corpus, which is $0.00027 per shell result sifted. The arithmetic above is shell results per session times that.

The ledger gives a second, independent arm on the same quantity and it is the more expensive one. 1,756 of its rows are real wire calls carrying a latency and a cost, at a p50 of $0.0000547 each, and the shipped splitter puts 8.7 candidate units in the average shell result of the corpus, one Jev call apiece. That is $0.000478 per shell result, 1.8 times the TOFU-217 figure, and it would make the median session $0.00096 and the worst $0.0196.

**Take the ledger arm as the ceiling and the TOFU-217 arm as the floor.** The ledger's rows are `tool_gate`, `inline` and `stop_check` states, not shell units, so its price per call is not this point's price per call. Both arms land under two cents in the worst recorded session.

## Milliseconds added per tool call

**838 ms, and it is not from the ledger.** The ledger has zero `shell_sift` rows, so the only recorded figure for this point is the TOFU-217 log of 2026-09-21, which measured 838 ms per shell result against a mean step of 5,520 ms, 15 percent.

What the ledger does bound is the shape of it. Over the 1,756 recorded wire calls a Jev decision comes back at a p50 of 352 ms, a p95 of 572 ms and a worst of 3,955 ms. A sift fans 8.7 calls out at `konst.SiftConcurrency` of 4, which is three waves, so a p95 wave puts 1,717 ms on the tool call and the recorded worst single call alone is 3,955 ms. **The p50 sets nothing here: the tail does, and the tail on one recorded call is four seconds.**

The number nobody can produce yet is the added latency as a fraction of the shell call's own duration. The sessions record `duration_ms` on every tool call, and `bench/corpus.RecordedCall` does not carry the field, so this package cannot read it without writing a second session walker. Naming it rather than duplicating the walker.

## Bytes not sent to the model

**54.0 percent of a shell result, which is 13.7 percent of every byte the model reads back from a tool.**

The 54.0 percent is the Jev arm's bytes saved from the TOFU-217 log, the run that kept 27 of 34 needles. The denominator is measured here: across 109 recorded sessions the model was handed 2,283,911 bytes of tool output, and 579,902 of them, 25.4 percent, were shell results.

Counting every resend, which is what actually reaches the model since each step re-sends the conversation, shell results are 6,331,877 bytes of 25,957,578, 24.4 percent, and the saving is 13.2 percent. The two agree closely enough that the resend weighting changes nothing about the decision.

**The denominator is tool output, not the whole context.** The prompt, the instructions and the assistant's own text are not recorded as bytes anywhere in `.tofu/sessions`, only as `prompt_tokens`, so the share of everything the model reads cannot be stated from the record and is not stated here.

## Shell results per session

Median 2, p95 20, worst 41, 419 in total over 109 recorded sessions.

37 of the 109 never ran a shell at all and pay nothing. Over the 72 that did, the median is still 2 and the p95 is 24. **The distribution is why the bill is small and why a ceiling is still worth setting: one session in ten runs twenty shells and one ran forty one.**

## What was skipped and why

- 243 of 2,999 ledger rows carry no `point` and were skipped: 223 of them are rule findings written by the rule engine and 20 are outcome rows. Neither is a Jev call.
- 1,000 of the remaining 2,756 carry a point but no wire call, a zero latency and a zero cost, because they are replays off the cache or a stop check answered without the wire. Counted, not priced.
- 1 entry under `.tofu/sessions` was skipped by the walker: `HEAD`, which is not a session file.
- No row was skipped for a missing field. Every recorded tool call carried `rendered_bytes`.

## What this report does not do

It does not switch the policy on. `library/tools/shell/rules/shell_sift@1.yaml` still reads `mode: shadow`, `TestTheShippedRuleIsShadowAndTheModelIsGivenTheOutputWhole` still passes, and the model is still given every shell result whole.

It does not rerun the Jev arm. The 79.4 percent needles kept, the 54.0 percent bytes saved, the $0.00027 and the 838 ms are all stated once, from the TOFU-217 log, and this ticket forbade a live call.
