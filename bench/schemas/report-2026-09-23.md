## What caching does to the number, from the recorded turn

`turn-18d7e7074db17e6c`, the tofu arm of the 2026-09-23 harness run, wrote 6654 cache tokens on step 1 and never wrote that block again across its 11 steps; every later step reads 12825 or more tokens back from cache instead. The computed schema block above, 4143 tokens, is about 62% of that one write, the rest being the system prompt and the project instructions this machine's own CLAUDE.md carries. Across the whole turn tofu was billed 7620 fresh input tokens, 12913 cache-write tokens and 91010 cache-read tokens: cache reads are 81.6% of the turn's billed input, the same order of magnitude as the harness report's own claude figure for the same task, 128,058 of 139,047, 92%, though the two arms are not the same credential kind and are not compared as a difference.

**The same pattern holds across every recorded turn old enough to carry cache fields.** 51 of 111 turns under `.tofu/sessions` carry usable cache accounting (the other 60 predate cache accounting or hold no steps, see the skip counts below); of those, 24 had at least one cache-write step, and 23 of those 24 ran a second model call and so read the write back at least once. Restricted to the 15 turns whose first write is at today's 18-tool scale, 14 of 15 ran a second call.

Over the 24 turns with a cache write, the cohort billed 103777 fresh input tokens, 675237 cache-write tokens and 5700621 cache-read tokens: 88.0% of everything billed across this whole sample was a cache read.

## The mechanism being costed

a tool_definition fetch: tofu sends a one-line name and one-sentence purpose for every tool up front, adds one new tool named tool_definition(name), and the model must call it once for each tool whose full schema it has not yet fetched, before that tool's first real use. Deferring means the schema bytes for a tool are not paid until the model has already decided it wants that tool, which does not remove the bytes, it moves them from the initial `tools` array into a later `tool_result` and adds a full model round trip in front of the tool's first real use.

On `turn-18d7e7074db17e6c`, the model called 5 distinct tools out of 18 offered: bash, edit, glob, project_report, write. Deferring would have cost 5 extra round trips, one per first use, against a catalog of one-line summaries costing about 360 tokens on every step instead of the full 4143, saving at most the 2873 tokens belonging to the 13 tools never called, paid once at cache-write price rather than not at all.

## Verdict

**Not worth building.** The schema block is written to cache once, on step 1, and 14 of 15 recorded turns whose first write matches today's tool count went on to a second model call and read it back instead of paying for it again; the schema's own share of that one write, about 4143 tokens, costs a fraction of a cent at cache-write pricing and nothing at all afterward. Deferring would trade that one-time, mostly-amortized cost for a mandatory extra round trip per distinct tool a task actually needs, which on the one turn measured here would have added 5 model calls to an 11-step, 65,678 ms turn to save tokens worth less than one of those calls costs in wall clock alone. The one case where deferring would pay is a turn that makes exactly one model call and never returns for a second, which was 1 of 24 turns in this sample: too rare and too cheap a miss to build a mechanism for.
