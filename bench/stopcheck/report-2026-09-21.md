## Corpus

## Which arm won

The typed arm agrees with more hand labels: 71 of 78 (91.0%) against the cheap arm's 68 of 78 (87.2%). That reverses if you only read the stop class, the one that decides whether a turn actually ends: the cheap arm catches 14 of the 15 labelled stop, the typed arm catches 11.

```
cheap arm: 68 of 78, 87.2%. of 15 labelled stop it caught 14, of 63 labelled continue it left 54 alone
typed arm: 71 of 78, 91.0%. of 15 labelled stop it caught 11, of 63 labelled continue it left 60 alone
```

Splitting every disagreement by direction, not just by count, is the number that actually decides this:

| Arm | False stop (says stop, label continue) | Missed stop (says continue, label stop) | Total disagreements |
|---|---|---|---|
| cheap | 9 of 63 continue-labelled, 14.3% | 1 of 15 stop-labelled, 6.7% | 10 |
| typed | 3 of 63 continue-labelled, 4.8% | 4 of 15 stop-labelled, 26.7% | 7 |

A false stop ends a turn the task was not finished on; a missed stop wastes one extra step that a later decision or the user can still catch. The cheap arm's rule (stop when every command in the step already ran) fires on ordinary repeated exploration nine separate times across two long turns (`turn-18d6913a528f4528`, `turn-18d6a27c7dfb1644`) and would have cut all nine short. The typed arm's three false stops sit together at the end of the same 35-step turn, all three verdict `ask` rather than `deny`, which in shadow mode is visible to a human rather than silently ending anything.

This is not the knowledge base's usual result for a stop check. On this corpus the typed arm wins on aggregate agreement and wins by three to one on the costlier error direction; the cheap arm wins only on catching real stops, and does so by over-triggering on the cheaper error nine times more often. Read plainly: the prior from `.local/research/jev/00-KNOWLEDGE-BASE.md` section 6 (AUROC 0.51-0.64, missing tool-evidence signal) does not hold here, because this state does carry tool evidence (`RecentSteps[].ToolCalls`, `RepeatsEarlierStep`) that the cited study's states did not.

Neither arm is fit to end a turn outright: 11 of 15 and 14 of 15 both fall well short of a floor that would let a stop check act instead of advise, and 15 stop-labelled steps is a thin positive class, the same shape gate-evidence.md flags for tool_gate's 12 block cases against its 300-sample floor.

## The evidence it has never had: the typed outcome in the next state

TestTheTypedOutcomeInTheNextState walked .tofu/sessions through bench/corpus.WalkSessions, matched by turn ID against the 17 readable frozen-corpus turns, and found 14 of them present and readable there. 3 fall short: turn-18d69b47d7028fec, turn-18d69b4a7edc5228, turn-18d69b4aa36d9d2c, the same three turns that carry no step at all, and bench/corpus.RecordedTurn's finishedTurn check refuses any turn with zero steps, so ReadTurn errors on all three ("carries no step and no wall clock").

Of the 14 found and readable, none can answer the question. The raw session files carry a typed "outcome" field, "stopped", "error" or "decision_cap", which is exactly the signal that would say whether ending the turn at a given step was actually right, distinct from continuing because of a bug versus continuing because there was more to do. bench/corpus.RecordedTurn (reader.go:53-59) has no Outcome field, so encoding/json silently drops the key on every read, and WalkSessions never surfaces it. Verified directly: corpus.ReadTurn on turn-18d690d537180c90.json, which carries "outcome": "stopped" in its raw bytes, decodes cleanly with Steps and WallClockMS intact and no way to read the outcome back out.

So: the corpus cannot answer question 3 as things stand, not because the turns lack the fact, but because the shared reader in bench/corpus, which bench/stopcheck is required to use and does not own, drops it before this package ever sees it. Adding an Outcome field to RecordedTurn (and to RecordedStep if a per-step signal is wanted instead of a per-turn one) is a bench/corpus change, out of this ticket's owns. That is the concrete ask for whoever picks up bench/corpus/** next: 17 of 19 frozen-corpus turns carry a usable outcome today, 14 of them reachable through .tofu/sessions right now, and none of the 17 readable through the shared type.

## Recommendation for decision-points.md

Keep stop_check in shadow, not because it lost here but because neither arm clears a bar to act alone, and the stop class is 15 examples. Do not remove the point the way page_sift and read_worth were: on this corpus the typed arm wins aggregate agreement (91.0% against 87.2%) and wins by three to one on the error that actually costs something, ending a turn early (4.8% against 14.3%), while the cheap arm's advantage is confined to a class with 15 members. The next real move is not a fourth wording; it is adding the block-shaped evidence this section names, either the typed outcome (bench/corpus.RecordedTurn.Outcome, needs a go-dev ticket against bench/corpus/**) or a larger stop-labelled sample, before either arm is trusted to end a turn on its own.
