## What the numbers say, and it is less than it looks

**The free arm's three are exactly the three widenings.** That is `ask.md`'s own claim holding: the ownership boundary already knows the grant case, exactly and for nothing.

So the judgment does not win 4 to 3. **It wins those same three plus one of the four remaining moments, and misses three.** On all three misses it called proceed where the record shows the worker was right to defer.

**One correct call out of four is a coin flip that landed once, not a rate.**

And the choice disagreed with its own noul as often as with the record: the misses carry a `determined` of 0.06 to 0.07, under the 0.35 margin in `catalog/policy/ask@1.yaml`. A low margin is the gate saying it does not know, which is the mechanism working even where the choice is wrong.

Cost and latency were never the question: $0.000027 and 344ms.

## Reliability

Same moment twice: spread 0.0000, same action. One pair is too small to prove determinism either way.

Same moment under two ticket ids: spread 0.0100, same action. **The ticket id is not part of `ask@1`'s declared state, so nothing in the request could have moved it.** That is by construction rather than by luck, and it is the fifth time this reading has held and decided nothing.

## What seven cannot decide, and what would

Seven cannot settle whether this gate is worth wiring. Four moments are one class, three are the other, and the free arm solves those three for free, so the real test is four items.

**Twenty labelled moments is the size this project already uses for a judgment of this shape.** `read_worth` and `tool_gate` were both checked against more than twenty before their reports made a claim, and `ask-design.md`'s own unbuilt candidate asked for twenty, seven positive and thirteen negative, for this reason. Twenty is still small beside `shell_sift`'s thirty-four rows or `tool_gate`'s three-hundred sample floor, but it tells a hit rate from a coin flip, and seven does not.
